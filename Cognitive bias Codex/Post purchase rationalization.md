
Tendency to justify a buying decision **after** making the purchase, especially when the purchase was expensive, uncertain, or involved competing alternatives.

You buy an expensive phone, then start telling yourself:

- “It has a much better camera.”
- “I’ll use it for several years.”
- “The cheaper model wouldn’t have been as good.”

Even if you later notice drawbacks, you may selectively focus on information that makes your purchase feel like the right decision.

**Why it happens:**

1. You make a choice between alternatives.
2. You experience uncertainty or regret afterward.
3. That discomfort creates cognitive dissonance.
4. You seek reasons that support your decision.
5. Your attitude toward the purchase becomes more favorable.

**In marketing:** Companies often reduce post-purchase dissonance through confirmation emails, warranties, customer support, positive reviews, and messages reinforcing that the customer made a good choice.
