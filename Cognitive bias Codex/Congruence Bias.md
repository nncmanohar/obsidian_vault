**Congruence bias:**

Tendency to **test a hypothesis by looking for evidence that is consistent with the hypothesis**, rather than looking for evidence that could _disprove_ it.

**Hypothesis:** “People who exercise regularly are healthier.”

You might investigate people who exercise and discover that many are healthy.

That supports your hypothesis—but it does not tell you whether exercise is the reason.

A stronger test would compare:

- people who exercise regularly
- people who don't
- while controlling for other relevant factors


**Hypothesis:** “My program is slow because the database is slow.”

You check database performance.

- Database is slow → “That's the problem!”

But you never check:

- network latency
- CPU usage
- memory pressure
- inefficient algorithms
- excessive API calls
- serialization overhead

The database may indeed be slow, but you haven't established that it is **the cause**. You only tested something that was consistent with your initial theory.



“What competing explanations are possible, and what test would distinguish between them?”
