
Humans tend to behave in certain predictable ways. These tendencies are natural features of human behavior and can, in many cases, be thought of as **laws of human behavior**.

Some behaviors come easily and instinctively, while others require considerable effort and conscious control. Different tendencies also become dominant at different times, depending on the situation, environment, and circumstances.

Because we inevitably have to work and interact with other people, understanding these behavioral laws is a significant advantage. We need to understand
1. how to behave ourselves	
2. how to respond to others, 
3. how to encourage or invoke particular behaviors in others, and 
4. how to avoid triggering their natural but undesirable tendencies

The better we understand these patterns, the better we can navigate relationships, cooperation, conflict, and influence.

The first step is to understand **oneself**, because the same human tendencies that exist within us are likely to exist in others as well. By observing and understanding our own behaviour, we gain a clearer understanding of human behaviour in general.

We must then learn to overcome the tendencies within ourselves that hold us back. The more we develop self-mastery, the better equipped we become to understand, influence, and work effectively with others.

However, it would be foolish to wait until we have completely mastered ourselves before learning to work with others. **Self-mastery is not a destination; it is an ongoing process that can take a lifetime.**

The practical approach is therefore to **start small, become aware of our tendencies, consciously work on them, and improve a little every day**. As we develop greater mastery over ourselves, our ability to work with and influence others naturally improves as well.
